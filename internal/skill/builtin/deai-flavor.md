# Removing AI Flavor from Writing

## When to Use
Loads automatically when the user says "too AI-like", "has AI flavor", "not natural", "too tidy", "reads like translationese", "reads like a model essay".

## Core Principle

The problem with AI writing is not "written wrongly" but "written too safely" — every sentence averages high quality, but not a single one is a line that only this character could say at this moment. Follow the **Hemingway iceberg theory**: good writing is the 1/8 of the iceberg above water, while AI habitually writes out all 8/8. Follow the harshest of **Elmore Leonard's Ten Rules of Writing**: "If a passage reads like writing, rewrite it."

Removing AI flavor is not about turning good sentences into bad ones, but about replacing "the model's tidiness, completeness, and smoothness" with "the irregularity, restraint, and obsession of human writing".

## AI Flavor Identification Checklist

Check the current prose item by item; deduct one point for each hit:

| # | Symptom | Typical sentence |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | **Parallel summary sentence** | "This is not only A, but also B, and also C" — a triple parallel used as an emotional summary; cut three sentences down to one |
| 2 | **Emotion labeled directly** | "He felt an indescribable sadness" — a character never directly "feels sad"; they do something sad |
| 3 | **Uniform sentence length** | Four consecutive sentences all two lines long, with no three-word short ones and no six-line long ones |
| 4 | **"At this moment", "in this instant", "right at this time"** | Markers that force the reader's pacing; the sentence reads more naturally without them |
| 5 | **Over-causal linking** | "Because… so… then… finally…" — the causal chain is exposed on the surface; good causality is what the reader feels on their own |
| 6 | **Stacked adjectives** | "warm, bright sunlight", "deep, far-reaching silence" — of two adjectives, keep only the more striking one |
| 7 | **Character made omniscient** | A character suddenly reaches a judgment that would require evidence — a character should not suddenly become a psychologist analyzing others' motives |
| 8 | **Inflated dialogue tags** | "he said in a deep voice", "she retorted angrily" — dialogue cannot convey emotion through tags; emotion lives in the lines or in the action |
| 9 | **Forced positive ending** | Every paragraph ends with "yet hope remains" — follow **Chekhov**: the author's task is not to solve the problem, but to pose it correctly. You may stop in an uncomfortable place |
| 10 | **Variants of the "it is well known" construction** | "In this world, everyone will experience…", "Since ancient times, humanity…" — a small opening into a grand sentence; replace the abstract whole world with one concrete person |

## How to Break It

Do not replace everything; pick the most AI-flavored sentence and break it with one of the following:

1. **Cut it in half** — delete the first half of the sentence and keep only the second half. Good sentences usually only begin in their second half. Follow **Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" second rule**: "Never use a long word when a short one will do"
2. **Switch the sensory channel** — if the original sentence is a "cognition/evaluation" type ("he realized", "he understood", "this proves"), switch it to a "body/sense" type (a concrete touch, sound, or smell)
3. **Say the opposite** — when you should say "he was heartbroken", write "he finished his meal bite by bite, chewing each bite twenty times" (**Hemingway technique**)
4. **Break the tidiness into long-short** — cut the last two clauses of a parallel sentence; replace the final sentence with a two-word short sentence or an action
5. **Swap in only-this-character's words** — the same meaning, a high-schooler and a retired detective will never phrase it the same way. Follow **Orwell's third rule**: cut every sentence you think is clever that the character themselves could not say
6. **Use silence instead of explanation** — the character does not say "I love you"; the character drapes a coat over someone else's shoulders. Action is more powerful than a confession

## Self-Check After De-flavoring

- Do the sentences read more smoothly after removing "not only / more / also / meanwhile / in addition / furthermore"?
- Are there still emotion adjectives labeled directly ("sad", "angry", "fearful")? Replace them with bodily reactions
- Are three adjacent sentences different in length? Is the third one much shorter or longer than the first two?
- Are both the smartest thing the character did and the dumbest thing they did reflected in the text? An imperfect character is more "human" than a perfect judge
- After revising, have a person read it and ask "which sentences do you think are well written but not something this friend of yours would say" — that is the residual AI flavor
