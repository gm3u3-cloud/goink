# Scene Beat Design

## When to Use
Loads automatically when the user says "write this scene", "this drags", "the scene lacks tension", "how do I write the beats".

## Workflow

1. **Fix the function** — each scene completes at least two things: advance the main plot + change the character's state (perception/emotion/relationship). Follow the **Scene-Sequel pattern** (Dwight Swain): first ask whether this scene is a Scene (action-oriented) or a Sequel (reaction-oriented)

2. **Choose the POV** — always choose, as the viewpoint, the character in this scene with the greatest emotional impact and the most drastic change of situation. If switching the POV barely changes how the scene feels, the POV is wrong. Follow the **MRU (Motivation-Reaction Unit)** "stimulus → instinct → thought → action" order, and at key moments give focus to the person reacting most intensely

3. **Write the entry** — the first sentence goes straight into conflict or suspense, no warm-up, no buildup. Three effective entries: ① in action ("the moment the blade came down") ② an abnormal detail ("he noticed an extra cup of tea on the table") ③ a contradictory state ("she smiled as she said the harshest thing")

4. **Escalating beats** — at least 3 **Try-Fail Cycles**: the character tries to reach the goal → is blocked → switches strategy → the situation worsens. Each beat puts the character further from the goal or at a greater cost. Follow the escalating pace of "catalyst → midpoint → all is lost" in the **Save the Cat beat sheet**; it applies at scene level too

5. **The turn** — at some moment in the scene, the character's situation changes irreversibly. Three types of turn: ① new information changes perception ② advantage reverses ③ the character makes a choice with no way back. If no turn happens at all, this scene can be deleted

6. **Exit hook** — end on a contrast of psychological state. Technique: the character believes some answer will bring relief, but it brings greater fear. Never end on the state of "everything has calmed down"

7. **Final-line check** — compare "she closed the door and left" with "she closed the door and found the key was inside". The aftertaste of the last sentence decides whether the reader turns to the next chapter

## Signal Check

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|------|------|------|
| Two people chat friendly and reach consensus | Lack of conflict | Give both sides hidden purposes; make the dialogue a game |
| Large inner monologue with no external action | Pure Sequel, no Scene | Interrupt with a concrete event; force the character to react |
| Ending naturally settles down | Lack of hook | Cut at the peak and leave an irregular thing protruding |
| Character state unchanged in the scene | No function | Delete it or merge it into another scene |
| The POV swap feels about the same | Wrong viewpoint | Find the person who is hurt most / cares most |
